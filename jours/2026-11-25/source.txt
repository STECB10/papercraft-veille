TITRE : Aortic Valve Stenosis Screening Using Deep Learning on Noninvasive Arterial Pressure Waveforms.
REVUE : JACC. Advances · 2026-11-20
DOI : 10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103475

RÉSUMÉ :
Background Aortic stenosis (AS) presents a significant health burden. Early identification of significant AS, defined as at least moderate AS, may facilitate timely confirmatory testing, referral, and management. Objectives This study sought to develop and externally validate a deep learning model applied to arterial pressure waveforms obtained from a noninvasive finger-cuff system to screen for significant AS, using transthoracic echocardiography as the reference standard. Methods This prospective international multicenter cohort study was conducted from December 2020 through July 2025. Participants underwent concurrent transthoracic echocardiography and noninvasive arterial pressure waveform acquisition. A 1-dimensional convolutional neural network was developed using the Cleveland Clinic development cohort and evaluated in a temporally distinct, multicenter validation cohort including sites in the United States, Spain, and the Netherlands. Results Significant AS was present in 307 of 3,634 (8.4%) patients in the development cohort and 241 of 1,336 (18.0%) in the validation cohort. In the validation cohort, the model demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.893 (95% CI: 0.876-0.910), with a sensitivity of 92.5% (223/241; 95% CI: 88.5%-95.2%), negative predictive value of 97.5% (706/724; 95% CI: 96.1%-98.4%), specificity of 64.5% (706/1,095; 95% CI: 61.6%-67.3%), and positive predictive value of 36.4% (223/612; 95% CI: 32.7%-40.3%). For severe AS, the area under the curve was 0.906 (95% CI: 0.890-0.922), with a sensitivity of 94.3% (183/194; 95% CI: 90.1%-96.8%), negative predictive value of 98.5% (706/717; 95% CI: 97.3%-99.1%), specificity of 64.5% (706/1,095; 95% CI: 61.6%-67.3%), and positive predictive value of 32% (183/572; 95% CI: 28.3%-35.9%). Conclusions Deep learning applied to arterial pressure waveforms obtained from a finger-cuff system serves as a promising screening test for significant AS.
