TITRE : Diagnostic Performance of Radiomics-Based Machine Learning in Differentiating Glioblastoma From Brain Metastasis: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
REVUE : Journal of medical Internet research · 2026-11-19
DOI : 10.2196/104060

RÉSUMÉ :
Background Glioblastoma (GBM) and brain metastasis (BM) are the most common malignant intracranial tumors in adults. Noninvasive differentiation between them remains challenging, as they frequently share similar imaging features. Although radiomics-based machine learning (ML) models show promise, systematic evidence of their performance remains limited. Objective We aimed to systematically estimate the diagnostic performance of radiomics-based ML models in differentiating GBM from BM and to explore multiclass classification involving primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL), thereby assessing their potential for clinical use. Methods We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library databases from inception to January 22, 2026, for studies reporting the diagnostic performance of radiomics-based ML models for differentiating GBM from BM. Multiclass studies involving PCNSL were also retained. Methodological quality was appraised via the Radiomics Quality Score 2.0. Binary diagnostic accuracy was pooled using a bivariate random-effects model, stratified by internal and independent external validation. Multiclass sensitivities and specificities were pooled using separate joint generalized linear mixed models. Physician assessments were evaluated as an exploratory benchmark. Results A total of 39 studies (31 binary and 8 multiclass), involving 11,558 patients, were included. For binary classification, pooled sensitivity and specificity were 88.9% (95% CI 84.1%-92.4%) and 83.0% (95% CI 76.8%-87.9%) in internal validation, and 83.8% (95% CI 77.4%-88.7%) and 80.7% (95% CI 71.1%-87.7%) in external validation, respectively. In multiclass analyses, pooled sensitivity was 84.4% (95% CI 72.4%-91.8%) for GBM, 70.6% (95% CI 54.4%-82.8%) for BM, and 75.0% (95% CI 62.7%-84.3%) for PCNSL, with corresponding specificities of 82.5% (95% CI 70.1%-90.4%), 88.8% (95% CI 81.9%-93.3%), and 94.4% (95% CI 88.4%-97.4%), respectively. Conclusions Radiomics-based ML models could distinguish between GBM and BM in the included cohorts. However, due to methodological limitations and scarce evidence, their generalizability and clinical utility remain unclear. Further multicenter prospective studies should conduct independent external validation in consecutive patients.
