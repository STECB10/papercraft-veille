TITRE : Predictive Modeling of Sarcopenia Using Machine Learning: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
REVUE : Journal of medical Internet research · 2026-11-23
DOI : 10.2196/91297

RÉSUMÉ :
Background Sarcopenia is an age-related syndrome characterized by muscle loss and functional decline, increasing fall risks. Early identification is crucial, yet convenient and accurate screening tools remain limited in routine practice. Machine learning (ML) models show promise for early sarcopenia detection, but their diagnostic performance and methodological quality have not been systematically quantified. Objective This review aimed to quantitatively evaluate the diagnostic performance of ML models for sarcopenia prediction, compare performance across ML algorithms, and identify key sources of heterogeneity and methodological limitations. Methods PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were systematically searched from inception to April 15, 2026. Eligible studies developed or validated at least one ML model for sarcopenia and provided sufficient data to reconstruct a 2×2 contingency table. Risk of bias was assessed using PROBAST (Prediction Model Risk of Bias Assessment Tool) developed by Moons et al. The primary analysis used a bivariate random‑effects (Reitsma) model to jointly synthesize sensitivity and specificity. Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) values were pooled using a univariate random‑effects model with the Hartung‑Knapp‑Sidik‑Jonkman (HKSJ) method, and 95% prediction intervals (PIs) were estimated. A secondary analysis incorporated all models using cluster-robust variance estimation (RVE). Subgroup analyses and meta‑regression explored heterogeneity. The certainty of evidence was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE). Results Twenty studies (20 prespecified index models) were included. The pooled AUROC was 0.89 (95% CI 0.82-0.93; 95% PI 0.54-0.98), with pooled sensitivity of 0.77 (95% CI 0.68-0.83; 95% PI 0.37-0.95) and specificity of 0.88 (95% CI 0.83-0.91; 95% PI 0.62-0.97). Gradient boosting had the highest pooled AUROC (0.92), but CIs overlapped across algorithms. Externally validated models had a pooled AUROC of 0.85, lower than that of internal ones (0.91). The secondary analysis of all 115 models yielded consistent results. Meta‑regression identified no significant covariates. PROBAST assessment revealed that half of the studies were at high risk of bias. Conclusions This review provides the first quantitative synthesis of ML‑based sarcopenia prediction models, with several innovations: (1) it provides algorithm‑stratified pooled estimates of diagnostic accuracy, (2) it formally quantifies performance across algorithms and addresses the unit‑of‑analysis issue through a 2‑tier approach, and (3) it quantifies the performance gap between externally and internally validated models. By identifying specific weaknesses-insufficient external validation, poor calibration reporting, and high risk of bias-this review offers a roadmap for improving future studies. Practically, these models may serve as cost‑effective prescreening tools. However, GRADE certainty was very low, with substantial heterogeneity, insufficient external validation, and high risk of bias, indicating that current models are not yet ready for routine deployment. Future research should prioritize rigorous external validation, transparent reporting, calibration assessment, and clinically implementable design.
