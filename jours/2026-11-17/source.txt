TITRE : Geographical targeting of active case-finding for tuberculosis in Pakistan using artificial intelligence software (SPOT-TB): a pragmatic, stepped wedge, cluster-randomised control trial.
REVUE : The Lancet. Global health · 2026-11-12
DOI : 10.1016/j.langlo.2026.104090

RÉSUMÉ :
Background Community-wide active case-finding is being increasingly implemented as a tuberculosis elimination intervention. However, conventional site selection strategies can result in low screening yields. We aimed to evaluate whether geographical targeting using artificial intelligence (AI) software can improve detection of tuberculosis during screening in comparison to conventional approaches for site selection, within the programmatic setting in Pakistan. Methods We conducted a pragmatic, stepped wedge, cluster-randomised trial across all four provinces of Pakistan, and was embedded within The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria-supported active case-finding activities implemented by Pakistan's National TB Control Program and private sector partners. 30 mobile x-ray van teams operating in 68 districts were randomised to transition from conventional site selection approaches (based on field team knowledge, experience, and historical data) to an AI-guided targeting strategy using the Mapping and Analysis for Tailored disease Control and Health system strengthening-Artificial Intelligence (MATCH-AI) software. All individuals attending community active case-finding camps were eligible for participation except those excluded as per the national guidelines. The primary outcome, camp positivity yield, was defined as the number of individuals diagnosed with bacteriologically confirmed tuberculosis per camp. The primary analysis was by intention to treat and camps conducted within a 5-km radius of AI recommended locations were included in a validated per-protocol analysis. This trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06017843, and has been completed. Findings Between Aug 1, 2023, and Sept 28, 2024, 3936 screening camps were conducted (2046 [52·0%] control, 1890 [48·0%] intervention), screening 269 254 individuals. In the intention-to-treat analysis, camp positivity yield was 7% higher in the intervention group, but not statistically significant (adjusted risk ratio 1·07, 95% CI 0·94-1·22; p=0·27). In the validated per-protocol analysis, the camp positivity yield was 32% higher in the intervention group (1·32, 1·12-1·54; p Interpretation AI-guided targeting was associated with substantially higher detection of bacteriologically confirmed tuberculosis during active case-finding in the validated per-protocol analysis and could improve efficiency of community-based tuberculosis case-finding interventions in high burden countries. Routine programmatic data did not capture all implementation or process-quality indicators, limiting assessment of implementation fidelity and variation across sites.
